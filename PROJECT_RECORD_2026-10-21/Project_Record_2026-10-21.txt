1. The short version

The goal, in the owner's words: "Increase an LLM's ability to 'Comprehend' and 'Understand' in a way
that improves communication." The Bible, fables and moral stories were training material, not the
goal.

How it was tested. Tiny language models (about 3.4 million numbers each, trained from scratch on the
King James Bible on a 4-core cloud computer) were scored on stories and verses they had never seen.
Each test had controls. Where possible, the pass/fail rules were written down before any result was
seen.

Strong and repeated:

-   Marking right and wrong in the training text helps. A tiny model then judges new stories better:
    about 69 right per 100, against about 50 with no marking (experiments 8 and 10). This was a side
    measure, not the pre-registered main one.
-   Understanding shrinks the exact seed (experiment 12, confirmed). The tiny Bible model regrew 623
    verses it had never seen (83,757 characters) byte for byte from a 12.2 KB seed: 85.4% smaller,
    and less than half of what xz (a standard compression tool) needs. Each rung of shared
    understanding gave a smaller seed. The principle is already published (Shannon, 1951 onward).
    The measurement here is the project's own.

Supported once: the owner's carried balance (the shifting word) beat a plain good/bad label only in
long stories, where the act had scrolled out of the model's view. That was about 59 vs 53 right per
100 (p = 0.004, meaning luck alone would give this about 4 times in 1,000), in a re-run after two
fixes, with about 65 of 100 pairs tied.

Not confirmed:

-   predicting consequences (chance every time);
-   Aesop morals carrying across cultures (no training set beat chance);
-   the shifting word making seeds smaller (they came out about 2% larger);
-   the shifting word beating the plain label in short stories (a tie).

The seed and graph method regrows any file exactly.

-   It shrinks patterns (99.99%), but not ordinary text (6-19%, where zip manages 70%) or random
    data (0%).
-   The owner's reported 98.9% is most likely explained by a shared library: naming an item already
    on the shelf. With the Bible on the shelf this measured 98.6%.

Running now: the first test of the owner's own recharge rule, "W1 = 17 + 4 Remaining", with the
owner's numbers 17, 32, 16, 34 and 47.

-   18 tiny models are being trained.
-   The test passed an independent review and two dry runs.
-   The full run started at 17:45 UTC on 21 October and takes about 4 to 5 hours. No results yet.

Queued (owner-approved):

-   the "2" test: numbers the model can read;
-   then the +/- agent, with the owner's scoring rule: many small negatives, fewer but larger
    positives, every input pulling a little negative, and the two sides balancing.

The honest limit. Everything here comes from tiny models. None of it changes how a large AI
understands anyone. The next real step needs outside help: more computing power, a large-model API
key (an access code for a large AI's paid service), or a fresh expert reading.

2. The goal, and how it was sharpened

2.1 The goal, in the owner's words

On 18 October (05:46 UTC) the owner wrote the objective down:

  "Increase an LLM's ability to "Comprehend" and "Understand" in a way that improves communication."

An LLM (large language model) is an AI that reads and writes text, such as Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. The owner said the same thing in other words along the way:

-   "I am trying to teach actual "comprehension" and "understanding" for A.I. not about people" (17
    Oct, 21:52).
-   "I want to improve AI compression and understanding" (20 Oct, 09:30).

Working definition used in every test: comprehension means applying meaning to a new situation the
model has never seen. Repeating memorised patterns (rote) does not count. Every test is therefore
scored on new material against a coin toss (50% = chance). Each one also has a control (the same
setup with the meaningful part scrambled) and pass/fail rules saved before any result is seen
("pre-registered").

2.2 The first framing, and why it was dropped

The work began with the owner's script, a "Contextual Resonance Engine". It spotted words like
guilt, regret and betrayal in a person's message, scored their moral and emotional "weight", and
picked a response stance such as "Atlas Protocol". The AI fixed its bugs and added 16 tests
(resonance/, commit d8ebeec; a commit is a saved, dated change in the repository). It also said
plainly that the script's "wavelength" was a fingerprint of the text, not a measurement. Keyword
matching is shallow, and labelling a user's words "sin" means judging the person.

Eleven minutes after the first message, the owner said the goal was the AI's comprehension, "not
about people". From then on the question was this: can a reader that has only words, like a person
born blind learning colour, build real understanding? And do rules, plus the human errors that break
them, help? The script stays in resonance/ as a record.

2.3 Moral content was training data, not the goal

The owner's theory was that rules plus human mistakes give meaning its weight, and that lessons told
as stories transfer further than lessons told as rules. So the tests used the King James Bible, folk
tales and Aesop's fables. By the second day the work had drifted: it chased moral-judging scores and
lost sight of communication. Three things pulled it back:

-   18 Oct, 05:46. The owner asked the AI to "ensure that your drifting does not pass to the next
    llm". The AI found the objective was not written anywhere up front.
-   The audit (second session, 18 Oct). "All 11 experiments measure moral judging of Bible-style
    sentences in a 3.4-million-parameter model. None measures communication, and none uses a
    real-size AI." It put the project at "about rung 1 of 5".
-   18 Oct, 15:29. The owner: "The moral aspect was merely training data that you drifted with." The
    AI rewrote the method without the moral content. The habits it named were: mark what matters,
    carry it forward, let clear moments reset the state and small details nudge it, and keep a
    running "state note" with "DECIDED:" and "REJECTED:" markers in a conversation.

2.4 The drift guard (experiments/HANDOFF.md)

The AI added an "Objective and drift guard" section to the top of the handoff file on 18 Oct at
05:47 (commit 2b3d28e). Every new session reads it first. It says:

-   The tests are proxies, not the goal. Never chase a benchmark number for its own sake.
-   The Bible, the fables and the moral content are training material.
-   Communication is the payoff: through COMMUNICATION_GUIDE.md, and through the carried-state idea
    (can a model keep track of what matters across a long exchange?).
-   Scale honesty: tiny-model results do not change how a large AI like Claude understands anyone.
-   Tone: calibrated and plain. No "this could be huge" unless a test says so.
-   The first framing (reading people) was dropped.

The AI named the three risks at the same time: proxy drift, scale drift and tone drift. The audit
prompt the owner approved repeats the guard: "If a step does not move us toward better
communication, say so before you do it."

2.5 Later sharpening (20-21 October)

-   Understanding as compression ("the greenhouse"). A model that understands text better needs a
    smaller exact "seed" to regrow it. That makes understanding one measurable number (experiment
    12).
-   Comprehension as behaviour. The owner's Gemini and Copilot test showed comprehension as things
    you can score: staying with what is given, saying what is unclear, asking before assuming,
    keeping unrelated memory out. The memory an AI carries in was read as the owner's "+4
    remaining".
-   "Nothing is useless." Every input is "+" or "-" information added to a running total, which is
    gauged only by its placement on a line. This became the queued "+/-" agent.
-   Still untested: communication itself. The conversation test (PLAN step 1b) has not been run, and
    no large AI has been used as the reader. That needs an API key the owner would add.

4. The owner's ideas, in their own words, and what was built from each

Each idea below gives the owner's words, the plain meaning, what the AI built and what the tests
found. Quotes are copied exactly as typed, typos included. "Message N" is the Nth message in the
numbered list of the owner's messages.

All tests used tiny models trained from scratch: about 3.4 million parameters, able to see the last

4.1 Starting point: comprehension for the AI, not reading people

The owner first pasted a script that scored "human error" in text: "Evaluate and enhance if
possible". The AI fixed its bugs (resonance/resonance_engine.py, 16 passing tests). Then came
message 5: "I am trying to teach actual "comprehension" and "understanding" for A.I. not about
people".

That set the objective. The script reads people, so it was dropped as the framing. Its "wavelength"
is a text fingerprint (a code computed from the text), not a measurement.

4.2 Rules plus human error as the source of meaning

-   Words (message 6, 17 Oct): "The rules are the secret of understanding. The things we do wrong
    naturally and have to be told not to do them is something ai must mimic to grasp reality as a
    human does." Also: "If a person has never seen (born blind) do the truly understand what color
    is?"
-   Meaning: understanding forms where rules meet the mistakes that break them. A reader without
    senses can still learn meaning from how words relate.
-   Built: experiments/bible_understanding.py (experiments 1-4), a word-meaning model of the King
    James Bible (31,102 verses). Then consequences_test.py and tiny_transformer.py (experiments
    5-6), at the owner's "yes try the consequences test next" and "yes try the tiny transformer
    next".
-   Results:

4.3 The shifting word (the owner's central idea)

-   Words (message 9, 17 Oct, 23:00 UTC):
    -   "I will create a new word and give it a variant in its token structure. The idea is based on
        a syllables rotation sequence rather than a logical one"
    -   "[w1=17 -<Next word following it equal to (-13)> = +4 Remaining] next emergence of the word
        [W1=17+4 -<next 2 words following equaling (00)>= 0 Remaining]"
    -   "it must reset to [w1=0] when a clear right or wrong choice exists. If wrong the sequence
        will begin with a value of (-17) and right a value of (+17)"
    -   The variations "to", "too", "two" and "2"
    -   The aim: "shift the "coin toss" towards the desired outcome"
-   Meaning: a made-up word carries a running balance from one use to the next. The words after it
    add to the balance or use it up, and what is left (the "Remaining") carries forward. A clear
    right or wrong resets it to +17 or -17. Its spelling changes with the balance, so a reader sees
    the state in the text. "Shift the coin toss" means beating a 50% guess.
-   Built: experiments/shifting_word.py.
    -   The word velanok has five spellings, rotations of sha-ru-vel-an-ok, running from anoksha
        (heavy wrong) through oksharu, velanok (unresolved) and sharuvel to ruvelan (heavy right).
    -   RESET = 17 on a clear choice: a Bible verdict phrase, or a verse among the 3% most strongly
        worded at either end. That makes 2,029 reset verses.
    -   Word values come from the Bible model's good/evil direction (steal -5, love +6).
-   What the AI changed, and why:
    -   The recharge ("W1=17+4") was switched off. With it on, the balance pinned at its cap and
        almost every verse got an extreme spelling. It was left as the switch RECHARGE, untested
        until the numbers test (4.5).
    -   A fade was added, SPEND = 0.5 per valued word, so the balance drifts back toward
        "unresolved".
    -   Limits were added: a cap at +/- 51 and a reset at each book start.
    -   Only spellings reached the model, never the number. Its reader sees letters and punctuation
        only (see 4.10).
-   Results:

  Experiment   Test                                           Result
  ------------ ---------------------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  7            20 story pairs, consequences                   68% vs 52% scrambled. Later shown to be luck
  8            100 new pairs, consequences (pre-registered)   Not confirmed: 51/100
  8            100 new pairs, judging (side measure)          67, 72, 67 vs scrambled 56, 42, 60 and unmarked 49, 60, 40
  10           Vs a plain good/bad label per verse            Tie: 68.7% vs 67.7%. Not confirmed
  11           Long stories, everyday act out of view         Supported once: 59, 56, 63 vs label 54.5, 51, 54 (p = 0.004). A re-run after two fixes; about 65 of 100 pairs tied
  11           Long stories, verdict stated                   Not confirmed: the label won
  12           Exact seed size, 623 unseen verses             Not confirmed: 3.45 bits per verse larger

-   Known defect: it "carries perfectly but updates poorly". Started on "unresolved", it copied
    "unresolved" all the way down.
-   Untested: whether the shared syllables matter (this needs a sub-word reader), and the recharge
    rule.

5.0 The resonance engine (resonance/)

What it did. On 17 October at 21:41 the owner pasted a short Python script and asked to "Evaluate
and enhance if possible". It looked for trigger words (guilt, mistake, sin, betrayal, heavy, hide,
secret, fear) in four groups: regret 0.5, burden 0.7, commandment breach 0.8, unforgivable 1.0. It
added the weights, capped them at 1.0, and picked a "stance" for a host AI. It also printed a
"resonance wavelength".

Faults, each confirmed by running it: exact words only ("mistakes", "hiding", "betrayed" scored 0);
negation ignored ("I have no guilt" scored 50%); one word always triggered "grace", so the middle
stance could never fire; the owner's example added up to 3.8, shown as 100%; "system_halt" did
nothing; the wavelength included the clock time, so the same text never gave the same result.

Fixed (version 2.0.0, commit d8ebeec, 21:43, 16 tests passing): word stems; negation within 3 words,
stopped by a comma or "but"; new weights (0.30, 0.30, 0.50, 0.80) combined as 1 - (1 - a) x (1 - b)
x ..., so the score rises without pinning at 100% (the example now scores 70%); all four stances
reachable, including a new "System Halt"; evidence with word positions; the same text always gives
the same wavelength; a privacy option to leave the raw text out.

Honest review (21:47): the wavelength is only a fingerprint of the text, not a measurement; nothing
used the output; keyword matching is shallow; labelling a person's words "sin" judges them.

Why the framing was dropped. At 21:52 the owner wrote: "I am trying to teach actual "comprehension"
and "understanding" for A.I. not about people". The engine reads people. It does nothing for the
AI's own understanding. The code stays in resonance/ as a record and no later experiment uses it. At
22:02 the owner asked whether someone born blind truly understands color, and proposed the Bible:
"The rules are the secret of understanding."

("sins as scarlet"): every color sat nearer purity than sin, mostly because of Leviticus purity
laws.

Status: worked; one run, 14 objects.

5. Every experiment (part B: the seed test, the graph method, the greenhouse ladder, the numbers test)

The fair seed test (SEED_TEST_2026-10-20)

Where it came from. On 20 October the owner described their "data seed": a short expression of
numbers and symbols, plus fixed grow instructions (add, subtract, multiply, restart or work
backwards) that both devices already hold, so only the seed travels. The owner reported "100%
reconstruction accuracy and 98.9% data reduction" in Copilot and Gemini chats. They also reported an
earlier 99.8% that "required huge files". The AI built an AI-free test that runs on any computer:
SEED_TEST_2026-10-20/seed_test.py.

What makes it fair:

-   Every byte counts. Seed and original are both counted as raw bytes, so syntax or encoding cannot
    hide size.
-   Exact means exact. The regrown file must match the original byte for byte, checked by SHA-256
    fingerprint (a short code that changes if a single bit changes).
-   No peeking. Each seed is grown by a separate program in an empty folder that holds only the
    seed. A chat, by contrast, can see the original earlier in the conversation.
-   Grow instructions counted once. The script plus the method file are shared in advance, and their
    size is printed.
-   Hard cases. The test includes random bytes, fresh files the builder never saw, and limits of 60
    seconds to plant and 10 seconds to grow.

Results (percent smaller; every method regrew every file exactly, 5 of 5):

  Test file                          Zip                 Graph: fit the shape   Graph: work backwards   Graph: fixed page
  ---------------------------------- ------------------- ---------------------- ----------------------- -------------------
  Repeating pattern, 120,000 bytes   99.8% (204 bytes)   99.99% (11 bytes)      99.99% (10 bytes)       0%
  Short text, 267 bytes              66.3%               64.4%                  63.7%                   -1.5%
  Bible text, 100,000 bytes          70.2%               19.3%                  6.2%                    0%
  Word file, 11,110 bytes            9.9%                1.5%                   1.9%                    0%
  Random bytes, 100,000              0%                  0%                     0%                      0%
  All five together                  57.7%               42.1%                  38.2%                   0%

Source: SEED_TEST_2026-10-20/results_graph_methods.txt.

The counting limit, in plain words. There are more possible files than short seeds. A 100-byte file
can be any of about 10^240 files, but there are only 256 one-byte seeds. So no fixed rules can give
every file its own short seed. A method that shrinks some files must leave others the same size or
bigger. Random data has no pattern to expect, so it never shrinks, with any method.

The graph method, built three ways

The owner's description (20 October):

-   "Use a graph system".
-   Points that are only "0" or "1", stacked from the work of solving an expression, make "a page of
    binary rapidly and it doesn't change".
-   "Library style factors called strawberry and orange verse cucumber".

Separate builder agents made the three closest readings:

-   Fit the shape (methods/graph_fit.py). Stretches that a small add/subtract/multiply expression
    regrows exactly (lines, curves, cycles) are stored as that expression. The rest is stored as it
    is (the cucumber).
-   Work backwards (methods/graph_backwards.py). Solve back from the data to the rule's unknowns
    with real algebra, and keep a rule only where it costs less than the bytes it regrows.
-   Find it on a fixed page (methods/graph_locate.py). One fixed expression grows an unchanging page
    of 0/1 points. The seed lists where each 16-bit piece of the file sits on that page. Its factors
    use the owner's numbers, for example 17 x 32 x 16 x 34 x 47 = 13,908,992.

Strawberry, orange and cucumber are placeholder names. The owner has not yet shared their own
factors. If they do, those factors can go in a new method file and face the same test.

The independent checks: all three PASS. Each checker re-ran the builder's numbers and matched them
exactly. Round trips (plant, then grow, then compare) all came back exact:

-   fit the shape: 881 cases;
-   work backwards: 469 cases plus 1,200 random mixed inputs;
-   fixed page: 334 of 334.

No method hides zip or any other compressor. On fresh files the checkers chose, the results matched
the table:

-   fit the shape: a 60,000-byte Bible slice 19.6% (zip 65.1%), Python 23.0% (zip 69.5%);
-   work backwards: 4.0% and 13.8%;
-   fixed page: 0% on every file.

The checkers had only minor notes. Two docstrings were slightly wrong. Fit the shape slows to about
50 seconds per megabyte. Work backwards misses repeats more than 255 bytes back. A damaged seed is
not detected. Full reports: SEED_TEST_2026-10-20/checks/graph_methods_reports.md.

What it means.

-   The seed works where the data is a pattern. A repeating pattern shrinks to 10 or 11 bytes, which
    beats zip. That is where the owner's 98.9% shows up.
-   Everyday text is not shaped like lines or cycles. It stays mostly cucumber, and zip does better.
-   The fixed page never saves space. A 16-bit piece can be any of 65,536 patterns, and naming one
    of 65,536 spots takes 16 bits, the same size as the piece. The published "pi filesystem" joke
    fails for the same reason.

Experiment 12: understanding as compression, the greenhouse ladder (GROW_UNDERSTANDING_2026-10-20)

The owner's question (20 October): "can I grow understanding and compression?" The owner told it as
a story: find the soil, build a greenhouse, set the humidity and the sun's rotation, check the air
and the bugs, then plant the seed.

The idea. Both sides share a greenhouse, knowledge both already hold. The seed carries only what the
greenhouse cannot predict. Every word is a coin toss. A greenhouse that understands tilts the coin
toward what really comes next, so a likely word costs a fraction of a bit. The owner's day-1 phrase,
"shift the coin toss towards the desired outcome", fits this unit well. The technical name is
arithmetic coding: seed size equals the model's surprise, so understanding and compression become
one number.

How it was run. The script experiments/understanding_compression.py ran for 23.9 minutes on 20
October.

-   The text: 623 held-out Bible verses (every 50th verse, never trained on), 83,757 characters.
-   The coder: an exact whole-number arithmetic coder.
-   The greenhouses: built from training verses only, and not counted in any seed. The tiny model is
    about 13.8 MB, so it pays off only when reused for many messages.

The ladder (same text; every seed regrown exactly):

The library result: 98.6% against the owner's 98.9%. With the whole Bible on the shared shelf,
naming one verse costs log2(31,102) = 14.9 bits, about 1.9 bytes, against 132.5 bytes for an average
verse. That is 98.6% smaller and exact. It fits "it required huge files": the library is the huge
file. The records call this a likely explanation, not a proven one. It only works for what is
already on the shelf. Understanding is what works on new text.

The mirror in the seed expression. In plain characters (* for the owner's multiplication sign, DIV
for their division sign) the expression is [+2*y(27/5.2+t=y/?) DIV (?\y=t+2.5\72)yx2]. Reverse the
left half and mirror each sign (/ to , ( to ), the multiplication sign to the letter x), and you get
the right half exactly. Only the leading + has no partner. This was checked by computer. Reading it
as "growing is planting run in reverse" is an interpretation.

seeds can be regrown later.

The numbers test (NUMBERS_TEST_2026-10-21)

The request (20 October, 19:09 UTC): test the numbers "17,32,16,34"=?0=-0=+0=00=["47,"="17+4"]. The
owner said they did not know what it meant, only that "the +4 was a way to direct you to a way to
try testing the numbers where I feel like they belong."

The reading. Three independent AI readers and a judge settled it before any training. Their
write-ups are in NUMBERS_TEST_2026-10-21/design/interpretations_and_design.md.

-   Taken as certain: the order 17, 32, 16, 34, 47, and the slot: the place of 17 in the owner's
    day-1 rule "W1 = 17 + 4 Remaining".
-   What the slot is: the charge the word gets each time it appears, with the leftover from last
    time carried on top. This recharge was switched off in every earlier experiment, so this is the
    first test of the owner's full rule.
-   Left untested: ?0, -0, +0 and 00 are not tested, because no one knows what they mean. Reading
    "47 = 17+4" as "47 goes in the slot" is a guess, and one version tests it.

The old recharge switch could not test the numbers. It spent too little per verse, so the balance
stuck at the cap (+/-51). Swapping in any of the owner's numbers gave 100.0% the same marked text.

The rule, per verse:

1.  The word is spelled from the Remaining (R), using the same five spellings as before, from
    anoksha (heavy wrong) through velanok (unresolved) to ruvelan (heavy right).
2.  If a direction exists, the next charge is added in that direction: W = R + charge.
3.  A clear right or wrong verse sets R to +17 or -17 and restarts the list.
4.  Otherwise the verse's valued words add their values to W, and W shrinks by 14.1% per valued word
    (the "drain"). The result is the new R, capped at +/-51.

The drain was the AI's choice, not the owner's. It is 0.141073 per valued word, worked out from the
10.9043 valued words in an average verse. It is set so the plain rule settles at exactly the owner's
numbers: W = 17 + 4 = 21, each verse uses 17, and 4 remain. The dry run confirmed "W = 21.000,
Remaining = 4.000". This is what stops the word sticking at the top.

The six versions (3 training runs each = 18 tiny models):

  num_seq        17 at a clear choice, then 32, 16, 34, 47, 17 ...   The owner's numbers, in order
  num_seqrev     17, then 47, 34, 16, 32 ...                         Does the order matter?
  num_const29    29.2 (the average of the five)                      Sequence, or just a bigger charge?
  num_charge47   47                                                  47 in the place of "17+4"
  num_drain0     0                                                   Stops the AI's drain from taking credit

The comparators are earlier models, reused unchanged: the current shifting word (the main one), the
plain Bible model, and label, carry3 and scrambled (description only).

Measures:

-   M1, judging (main): 100 new story pairs, one right and one wrong.
-   M2, long stories: the act scrolls out of the model's 64-word view.
-   M3, exact seed size: bits to regrow the 623 unseen verses exactly. Run 0 must really regrow

Pass/fail rules, written before training (top of experiments/numbers_test.py):

-   Tests: one-sided: sign tests (counting wins and losses pair by pair) for M1 and M2, and a
    100,000-flip permutation test (reshuffling to see how often luck matches the real gap) for M3.
    Holm correction at 0.05 within each group, a guard against lucky wins across many comparisons.
-   Groups: each version against the shifting word (F1) and against the plain model (F2). The
    owner's numbers (F3): N1 num_seq beats num_charge17, N2 beats num_seqrev, N3 beats num_const29,

    -   "The owner's numbers matter" = N1 passes.
    -   "The sequence itself matters" = N1, N2 and N3 pass.
    -   "47 helps" = N4 passes.
    -   "The recharge rule helps" = num_charge17 passes F1, and is credited to the recharge only if
        R1 also passes.
-   Other outcomes: a pass on M2 or M3 alone is a "secondary sign". Anything else is "not shown at
    this scale", never "disproved".
-   Expectation written in advance: ties with the shifting word on M1, as every marker method has
    tied so far.

6. Changes, adaptations, mistakes and corrections

This section lists every place the work moved away from the owner's exact words, every fix made
after a problem was found, and every mistake the AI made. They are stated plainly so the rest of
this record can be trusted.

6.1 Changes the AI made to the owner's ideas, and why

-   The first framing was dropped. The first script (resonance/resonance_engine.py, 17 Oct) scored
    the emotional "weight" of a person's message using keywords. When the owner said "I am trying to
    teach actual 'comprehension' and 'understanding' for A.I. not about people", the work moved to
    the AI's own comprehension. The script stays as history. Its "wavelength" is only a text
    fingerprint, not a measurement.
-   Comprehension became a measurable stand-in (a proxy). Tiny models were scored on judging right
    and wrong in new stories they had never seen, against a coin toss. The proxy is not the goal
    (see the drift guard in experiments/HANDOFF.md).
-   The placeholder word became five spellings. The owner's "velanok" became five rotations of the
    syllables sha-ru-vel-an-ok: anoksha (heavy wrong, balance -12 or lower), oksharu (-11 to -4),
    velanok (unresolved, -3 to 3), sharuvel (4 to 11), ruvelan (heavy right, 12 or higher). The cut
    points are the AI's choice.
-   Word values were learned, not hand-set. The owner's example gave one word a value of -13. The AI
    took values from a good/evil direction in a word-meaning model (for example steal -5, adultery
    -10, mercy +5, love +6). That direction was built from four seed words per side, so it is a mild
    form of supervision.
-   SPEND = 0.5. Each valued word spends 0.5 of the balance toward zero, following "the next words
    use up the remainder". The rate is the AI's choice.
-   "A clear right or wrong choice" became a rule. Clear choices are the Bible's own verdict
    phrases, found with a text-matching pattern ("did evil in the sight of the Lord", "did that
    which was right"), plus the 3% most strongly worded verses at each end. 2,029 verses reset the
    balance to -17 or +17.
-   A cap and book resets. The balance is capped at +/-51 (3 x RESET) and resets to 0 at each book.
    The owner never asked for a cap.
-   RECHARGE was switched off. Re-adding 17 at every appearance ("W1 = 17 + 4") was coded as a
    switch and left off, because with it on the balance climbed to the cap and stayed there, so
    almost every verse got an extreme spelling. As a result, the core of the owner's day-1
    arithmetic was never tested in experiments 7 to 12.
-   Only 17 was used at first. The other numbers (32, 16, 34, 47) were set aside on the argument
    that only ratios matter (experiments/README.md, experiment 7). The numbers test reverses this.
-   The word-level tokenizer hid two things. The tokenizer [a-z]+|[.,;:?!] reads only whole words
    and punctuation. So the five spellings are five unrelated tokens, and their shared syllables are
    invisible. And digits are dropped: the model never saw "2" or "17". The number form of the
    owner's "to / too / two / 2" idea never reached the model. The "2" test is queued; a sub-word
    test is in experiments/PLAN.md.
-   The numbers test's drain. To test the charge slot without pinning at the cap, the AI replaced
    the flat spend with a proportional drain: each valued word shrinks the balance by 14.1% (DRAIN =
    0.141073). It was set so the owner's plain rule settles at exactly 17 + 4 = 21 in ordinary text,
    leaving 4. Other AI choices: the word is spelled from the Remaining before the charge, and the
    charge follows the direction of the last clear choice. A control (num_drain0) was added so the
    AI's drain cannot take credit for the owner's rule. The zero symbols ?0, -0, +0, 00 are not
    tested, because the owner has no meaning for them yet. Reading "47 = 17+4" as "47 goes in the
    slot" is a guess, tested by num_charge47. Every dropped option is listed in
    NUMBERS_TEST_2026-10-21/design/interpretations_and_design.md.
-   Graph-method placeholders. "Strawberry", "orange" and "cucumber" are placeholder names in the
    seed-test code, because the owner has not yet shared their own factors. Three readings were
    built: fit the shape, work backwards, and find it on a fixed page.

12. Plain-language glossary

-   LLM (large language model): a large AI that reads and writes text, such as Claude, Gemini or
    Copilot.
-   Tiny model / tiny transformer: the small model trained from scratch here: 3.4 million
    parameters, 4 layers.
-   Parameter: one adjustable number inside a model. Training tunes them.
-   Transformer: the model design behind modern AIs; it predicts the next token from the tokens
    before it.
-   Token: one piece of text the model reads, here a whole word or a punctuation mark.
-   Tokenizer: the rule that cuts text into tokens. Here [a-z]+|[.,;:?!], which drops digits.
-   Sub-word tokenizer: cuts words into shared pieces, so related spellings share parts. Not yet
    tried.
-   Context window: how far back the model can see: 64 tokens here (128 in the Aesop test).
-   Training step: one small update of the model. Most models here had 1,500 steps (the two small
    Aesop models about 275; dry-run models 5).
-   Seed (random start): the number that fixes a model's random starting point. Seeds 0, 1, 2 are
    three independent runs.
-   Seed (data seed): the owner's idea: a tiny message that regrows exact data using shared grow
    instructions.
-   Greenhouse: the owner's word for the shared grow instructions, such as a trained model both
    sides hold.
-   Embedding / word vector: a list of numbers standing for a word's meaning, learned from which
    words appear together.
-   PPMI: a score for how much more often two words appear together than chance would give.
-   SVD: a method that squeezes a large table of numbers down to its main directions (100 here).
-   Held-out: text kept out of training and used only for testing. Here every 50th verse.
-   Control: a version identical except for the idea being tested.
-   Scrambled control: the shifting word with spellings chosen at random: same extra word, no
    meaning.
-   Shuffled stories: stories with sentences shuffled; same words, broken event order.
-   Baseline: the plain model with no marker.
-   Plain label: the established method: each verse marked good or bad on its own, no carry.
-   Shifting word: the owner's made-up word whose spelling shows a running balance.
-   Remaining: what is left of the balance after a verse; it carries to the next appearance.
-   Reset: a clear right or wrong sets the balance to +17 or -17.
-   Clear choice: a verse that triggers a reset (verdict phrases, or the 3% most strongly worded at
    each end).
-   Regex: a text-matching pattern, used to find verdict phrases.
-   Spend: the old rule: each valued word moves the balance 0.5 toward zero.
-   Drain: the numbers-test rule: each valued word shrinks the balance by 14.1%.
-   Recharge: re-adding a charge (17, or the owner's numbers) each time the word appears.
-   Cap: the limit on the balance, +/-51. "Pinned" means stuck at the cap.
-   Manipulation check: a check before training that a rule really changes the text (here: no more
    than 5% at the cap; every spelling on at least 3% of verses).
-   Dry run: a fast run of every step with tiny settings, to test the machinery only.
-   Pre-registration: pass/fail rules saved to git before any result is seen.
-   Chance / coin toss: 50% right by guessing between two options.
-   Tie: the model scores both stories exactly the same; counted as half right.
-   Greedy marker choice: the model always writes its single most likely spelling; may hide small
    updates.
-   Sign test: counts pairs where one method won and the other lost, and asks if that split could be
    luck.
-   Permutation test: reshuffles the labels many times (100,000 here) to see how often luck matches
    the real gap.
-   p-value: the chance of a result at least this strong if there were no real effect. Below 0.05
    counts as a pass.
-   Holm correction: a stricter bar when several tests are run at once, so one lucky pass is not
    over-read.
-   Confirmed / supported once / not confirmed / untested / running: passed a pre-registered test /
    passed once, after a fix or without a fresh repeat / did not pass / not yet tried / started,
    with no results yet.
-   Arithmetic coding: turns a model's predictions into bits; good predictions mean fewer bits.
-   Bit: one 0 or 1. "Bits per character" measures how tightly text is packed.
-   Entropy: the true amount of surprise in data; no exact method can go below it on average.
-   Kolmogorov complexity: the length of the shortest program that regrows a file. It cannot be
    computed in general.
-   Pigeonhole / counting limit: there are fewer short seeds than files, so no method shrinks every
    file, and random data never shrinks.
-   SHA-256 fingerprint: a 64-character code computed from a file; any change gives a different
    code.
-   Byte for byte / exact regrowth: the regrown file is identical, checked by fingerprint.
-   Thread: one of the processor's lanes of work. A different thread count can change tiny rounding
    and break exact regrowth.
-   zip / xz: standard compression tools, used for comparison.
-   Drift / drift guard: sliding from the goal toward the measuring tool; the guard is the written
    check against it.
